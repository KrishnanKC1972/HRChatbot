Q: An employee wants to know if they can encash 15 unused annual leave days at year end. What is the policy?
A: Annual leave encashment at year end is allowed for up to 10 unused days, subject to policy cap. 15 days would exceed the limit; the employee can encash 10 days and carry over the remaining 5 days per carryover limits.

Q: Can two colleagues donate leave to a third who is facing a serious medical condition? What are the rules?
A: Yes, employees may donate accrued annual leave to another eligible employee experiencing a serious medical condition. Donors must retain minimum balance (typically 40 hours annual leave). Donations are in 4-hour increments; both parties can remain anonymous if they consent. Transferred leave is not paid out on termination.

Q: A female employee is expecting and has been with company 11 months, 1300 hours. Is she eligible for maternity leave and how much?
A: Maternity leave eligibility typically requires 12 months service and 1250 hours in past 12 months. At 11 months and 1300 hours, she may not yet meet standard eligibility under federal law; however, state laws may be more generous. Check applicable state PFML and company policy - company may provide job-protected leave beyond legal minimums in some cases.

Q: How does paternity/adoption bonding leave work? Can it be taken intermittently?
A: Eligible employees (12 months, 1250 hours) get up to 12 weeks job-protected bonding leave within 12 months of birth/adoption/foster placement. It can be taken continuously or intermittently with manager approval and policy compliance. First 6 weeks may be paid at 100% for non-birthing parents depending on policy; otherwise unpaid unless supplemented.

Q: An employee resigns voluntarily with proper notice after 5 years of service. What happens to unused leave?
A: Upon voluntary resignation with 2+ weeks notice and minimum service, up to 50% of unused accrued annual leave may be encashed. Upon retirement (age 55+ with 10+ years), 100% is paid out. Involuntary layoff with no cause also gets 100% payout. Sick leave generally not encashable.
